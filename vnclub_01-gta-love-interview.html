QUESTION 01

What does the title "GTA LOVE" mean to you, and what story or feeling were you trying to capture with the song?

After GTA LOVE release, I had the chance to explore more of Grand Theft Auto. I've clearly liked what I've seen; those community edits were a fit with the track's visual aesthetic.

As for GTA LOVE, I gotta say some girls were into GTA way too much, and it got me thinking, you know what I’m saying? I mean, does this sense of love even exist? That’s got me questioning it in the first place. It could've been love. After all, GTA or LOVE, these aren't different cuts. Like any other assumption, this is also from this culture. See, symbolically GTA LOVE is a story about a thug outliving his doubts in a relationship, once told. For some of them, this is "unfamiliar", while others got their safe spot, as a matter of fact, there has to be a contrast for something you feel "safe enough." It's okay to feel lost, as the only way get outta there is by experiencing all the way through.

And the ironic part is, I could already see it coming. I was just there, wondering, “Where was my mind at?”

QUESTION 02

Walk us through how the "GTA LOVE" video came together: whose concept was it, where did you shoot, and what moment from the set stays with you?

Hahaha, yeah. To be honest, I should've kept it in mind while the song was in progress but it took a lot more than it might seem. At this point, gotta give all the flowers to 9ojeez9. Arranging things frame by frame, constructing scenes from scratch; it’s just ahead of its time… We're living in 9ojeez9’s world already, shoutout, man. It's still unbelievable how it all works out. Who knows what got next? I just love seeing 9 in the Ankara scene. We've already released “HVI” and “BIEARTH” together, and there are dozens of other collaborations waiting in the vault. Maybe we get into that on another one.

I was actually in mood to shoot something, especially in my neighborhood. Unfortunately due to our pretty busy schedule, the idea of making it real always remained general, so we never actually started. Even so I’ve imagined GTA LOVE music video being quite vivid, on its own Vice City-inspired, purple-blue neon aesthetic, which was also used in official materials. That's what makes an archive worthwhile to me. Even without a music video, it has built a pretty rich visual catalog through visuals, edits, archives and collages.

If GTA LOVE had a music video, it’ll be the Launch Night for sure—that day was a GTA LOVE music video. I was in Istanbul on that time, working for the GTA LOVE's Premiere Night at FLINTA. We did catch a vibe right there. The night was just right. Their team was also involved in the process throughout the event too. Everyone was interested and enthusiastic about it. We were just having a good time, while making sure all the preparations were going all right beforehand. It had felt premium in many ways. It wasn't a crowd-based experience but hey, it was definitely something peaceful for real. It got moody, disco-driven and surprisingly low-pressure time to time. Played some unreleased too, though. For people who’s missed, I'd definitely like to see y'all in another one someday. Looking back, there gon’ be only one GTA LOVE Premiere. Still, anyone with access to the song can experience it again. There are no rules on that one; whatever makes you comfortable. Maybe just the right chemistry led us to use one of the iconic symbols there, the Galata Tower as GTA LOVE's visual aesthetic. I mean, who would have guessed that one? Bottom line, I’m grateful for what we had in there. I wanna thank both FLINTA ISTANBUL and VN Studios for all the valuable contributions.

I had a really great moment there, I remember when I get a chance to look out over the beautiful view around and it was fireworks all over in the sky. That was a surprise. Not everyone gets to be that lucky, so gotta be grateful for everything.

Funny moment I had was bringing my own champagne glass to the venue. See, I was out of town, and I got my fit was somewhere else so I was carrying a backup bag everywhere just in case. After I got changed, I went straight to the venue. Party got lit up and when the champagnes was being served, they said that there were no extra glasses left because all had been served. I got literally out of glass on my own appearance. Luckily, I happened to have a champagne glass in my bag, so I used that one. It all came out of the dome.

QUESTION 04

Where did you grow up, and how do that place and the people around you come through in your music?

I grew up in Ankara, between Çankaya and Mamak, back and forth. I've basically been in the same place since day one. My family took all the credit for me; I don't have to worry about that so much. My whole childhood played out across different streets in my city, just hanging around. I'd say I couldn't have done more for my city. I done love every corner of it, as I didn't get to live in all of them but I’d love to show as each someday.

Being part of an extra-oriented scene has definitely effected the way I see things. It’s about giving something for the city, and taking something from it back. A Lot of my creations comes from the environment I grew up in and the people I crossed paths. All might have influenced my point of view: being close to this atmosphere and the untold stories about it. Man, I love bringing it up. Damn, it's so peak for me. Always down for south... That’s where my soul belongs.

There's a lot going on about Ankara, It's layered, raw textured, and just whole lotta fabric. You may never know what might come out. Also hard to not mention, it’s only visible when you’ve actually inside, you could call it Wakanda. The city known for its people whose approach unique. This are go far beyond the music. There are also many examples of Ankara's influence, but that's not the subject of this interview. Maybe another time. One thing for sure: Ankara has it’s own thing. We call it “06”, I don't expect y’all to understand every part of it, either. If y’all ain’t so, maybe that's how it was supposed to be. I be definitely a good tour guide for Ankara, though. I could talk about it forever and never get tired. The point is, you couldn’t have defined Ojee Ka$h without Ankara.

QUESTION 05

How did the name Ojee Ka$h come about, and what does it say about who you are as an artist?

It’s funny because, see, at first, neither “Ojee” nor “Ka$h” had to do with my artistic purpose. To get into it real quick: shoutout to my high school friends, how everybody used to call me “Ojee.”—And I was him, you know? Although I've been hearing that name since I was born. It literally came out of nowhere. Maybe it was just an easy to pronounce, or they were clowning on me. I don't know. Either way, that’s me right there. Ojee didn't turn into as an artist name when I became an artist. Ojee was already a name with me as long as I can remember.

On the other hand, “Ka$h” comes whole different situation. Back then, it was connected to my business, getting strict cash, catching up with those loads, you know just being a middle cash boy. When I was get down with my cousins. They used to call me and all that, somehow it fit. There were different reasons behind it, partly with that whole wishing for abundance. Even so, I've got nothing to complain about; I mean, isn't that what we're doing right now?

All the jokes aside,

When you use a name which already you, there's gon’ be conclusion and some confusion along the way. When I'm on stage, I'm Ojee. When I’m at my personal, I'm still Ojee. It's directly connected to my personal life, directly connected to me. You have to deal with those things effectively. Because at the end of the day, you have to know how to introduce yourself properly first. I’d describe myself as a “Visionary Designer,” “Fashion Savant” rather than just a vocalist. I've involved in different stages, and it’s all more of a piece of tailored work to me. I think this names itself sits underneath all of these things and contains all of them for now.

QUESTION 06

What first pulled you into making music, and what keeps you going on the days when it feels hardest?

Let me put it straight: I was taught that music comes naturally. I’ve raised been told “if you don't have it as your birth right, then it's not your thing.” So if I wasn't believed that I’m naturally talented, I wouldn't had myself at this like that in first place. It's that no one else could really say what music has overly become in my life, as some other kid growing up in Southside; I used to see myself as one with music. It was there at my lowest point, when I was going through a lot. The first time I made something you might call it ‘music’ was back in 2018–2019. Before that, I was expressing myself through the way I talk or how I carry on. That’s my biggest inspiration right here, the one or two things I value most in my life: to inspire or encourage them keep going. That’s my biggest win-win right here and that’s when I started to create things and I couldn't have done all this without relying on the things really making me up; otherwise, I wouldn't still be counting right now, but it is what it is.

All I’m saying is: Look, this could be anyone who’s been through hard times. People might tell you about being too 'dramatic' or make fun of it when there was literally nothing funny. That doesn’t make you insignificant or make you special. You do. You really need to know how to look forward. And remember, if there's no one else around, that's when you'll feel the light. And if you ever feel that light, that's when you walk to it. Never be scared to walk alone.
